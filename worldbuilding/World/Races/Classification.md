Four tiers, and one question decides which one a thing belongs to: **what is inherited, and by whom?** A trait that lands on one person is a Mutation. A trait a family keeps is a Clan. A trait a whole population is simply born with is a Racial Subtype. A body plan that no longer needs its parent species to describe it is a Species.

Population size settles exactly one of those boundaries — Clan to Racial Subtype — and even there it is only half the test. Nothing else on this page is decided by counting people.

## Mutation

A **Mutant** is an individual occurrence, not an inherited classification. It does not matter whether some unrelated person on the other side of the world has developed the same thing. **Heavenly Restrictions**, **Dragon Slayers**, [[Vampires]], [[Fullbringer|Fullbringers]], [[Benders|Kinetics]], and [[Quincy]] can each arise independently in people with no relation to one another, and every one of those instances is its own Mutation.

The alteration may be spontaneous, externally triggered, or the result of surviving some transformation. It may also be **heritable** — that changes nothing about the original. Once a Mutant has descendants who consistently inherit the trait, those descendants can form a [[0 Clans|Clan]]; the ancestor stays a Mutant.

So several unrelated Dragon Slayers are all Mutants even when their abilities are indistinguishable from one another. They are separate instances of the same possible alteration, not members of one biological population.

See [[0 Mutants|Mutants]].

## Clan

A **Clan** is any socially recognized lineage or extended kinship group that preserves something across generations. *What* it preserves is irrelevant to the classification: a Mutation or inherited physical trait, a [[Nen Ability|Nen ability]] or unusual aura structure, a secret technique, a fighting style, a profession, a religious office, a political identity, or any combination.

The [[Uchiha]] are a Clan because they are a connected lineage inheriting the Sharingan and the techniques built around it. The [[Yamanaka]] are a Clan because they preserve their techniques and family identity, whether or not those techniques are genetically inherited at all. A Dragon Slayer family whose children keep inheriting the transformation is likewise a Clan.

**Size is not a disqualifier.** A Clan of hundreds of thousands is still a Clan, so long as membership stays tied to a shared genealogy, house identity, adoption structure, or inherited institution. A single Dragon Slayer family remains a Clan even if every child born into it inherits the condition — dominant inheritance makes a trait stable, it does not make a family a population.

See [[0 Clans|Clans]].

## Racial Subtype

A **Racial Subtype** appears when the variation is no longer meaningfully contained inside one Clan. Its members remain [[Humans|Human]], but they carry a stable secondary biological or aura pattern that has become *normal* across an entire population. The trait has stopped being "what this family inherits" and become "what people from this population are born as."

Five conditions:

1. **Multiple reproductively independent families.** They may share an ancient founder, but they no longer operate as one extended household or one socially coherent Clan. A person can belong to the subtype without belonging to any founding Clan.
2. **The traits breed reliably.** Two members produce a child who almost always expresses the essential traits. Individual variation is fine; the underlying pattern must be predictable.
3. **No trigger required.** A Dragon Slayer family whose every descendant must eat dragon flesh or undergo a ritual has not made a subtype. Descendants *born with* the organs and aura pathways have.
4. **Self-sustaining.** The population continues through births among its own members. It need not be isolated or forbid marrying out — it must simply not depend on constant reproduction with ordinary Humans to keep existing.
5. **More than one narrow ability.** A subtype carries a connected package — development, physiology, appearance, aura, senses, lifespan, environmental adaptation. A single inherited Nen ability stays a Clan bloodline.

### The demographic floor

Numbers alone never decide the classification, but a floor keeps one unusually large family from qualifying by sheer headcount:

| Requirement | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Effective breeding population | **1,000** reproductively mature members |
| Independent lineages or communities | **5** socially independent networks |
| Generations of consistent inheritance | **10** |
| Repeated exposure, ritual, or intervention | **none** |

The thousand is an *effective breeding population*, not a census — children, the infertile, and those who never reproduce do not count toward it. It is enough to prove the population survives beyond one household, escapes a narrow founder bottleneck, and develops internal variation without losing its defining traits. Most recognized subtypes will run to tens or hundreds of thousands; a thousand breeding members is only the lower boundary.

The five-lineage rule does not demand unrelated ancient ancestry — most subtypes descend from common founders. It demands that the population has separated into several independent genealogical networks that no longer add up to one meaningful Clan.

> A Dragon Slayer family of three hundred is a Clan. A Dragon Slayer dynasty of ten thousand that still recognizes one genealogy, one leadership, and one family identity is *also* a Clan. Five independent Dragon Slayer populations, thousands of breeding members between them, naturally producing Dragon Slayer children for many generations, are a Racial Subtype.

See [[0 Racial Subtypes|Racial Subtypes]].

## Species

A **Species** is not determined by population size or by a single dominant trait. It is determined by the existence of a distinct **developmental baseline**.

"Dominant gene" is the wrong frame here — genetic dominance only describes which version of a gene gets expressed, and a dominant mutation can spread through millions of Humans without creating anything new. What matters is the dominant *biological template*: the collection of traits that tells a developing child what kind of organism it is.

- A Human child inherits the Human baseline — skeleton, organs, nervous system, lifespan range, reproductive pattern, aura configuration, body plan. Extra traits layer on top; the child still develops fundamentally as a Human.
- A Racial Subtype has a *modified* version of that baseline. Gills, unusual eyes, greater size, specialized aura nodes, altered senses — ordinary Human development still runs underneath.
- A Species has its **own integrated baseline**. Its distinguishing characteristics affect the whole organism rather than one bloodline feature, and its members reliably produce children who develop according to that complete pattern.

Which reduces to three sentences:

| Tier | The claim it makes |
|---|---|
| Clan | "Our family carries this." |
| Racial Subtype | "Our population is normally born this way, but we remain Human." |
| Species | "This is the basic kind of organism we are." |

### When a subtype becomes a species

Only once the parent species' template stops being sufficient to describe the population. In practice that means several connected differences at once: a distinct developmental process, a different default aura architecture, widespread anatomical or physiological change, its own reproductive baseline, traits shared by nearly every member, predictable inheritance of the whole biological package, and adaptation to a distinct ecological or natural-energy niche.

Even then it is gradual. There is no generation in which everyone stops being Human, and scholars argue about the classification for as long as the population can still have children with Humans.

**Reproductive compatibility does not decide anything in this world.** The [[Angels]], [[Devils]], and [[Ancient Giants]] were compatible enough to eventually produce [[Humans|Humanity]] while being three separate species — biology and aura here do not work like real-world genetics. What decides species status is *which developmental template governs the child.*

A [[Merfolk|Fish-Man]] and a Human can have a child carrying both templates or one dominant one; that does not make Fish-Men a Human Clan, because a Fish-Man's difference is organism-wide — respiration, musculature, senses, skin, pressure tolerance, aura circulation. An [[Uchiha]] and an ordinary Human have a child who is fundamentally Human and may or may not inherit the Sharingan system. One is a species; the other is a family trait.

Note also that a species can decline to fifty survivors without ceasing to be a species. Numbers help prove a subtype has gone population-level; they never determine what kind of organism someone is. (Population *is* what sorts a species between [[0 Current Species|Current Species]] and [[0 Secondary Species|Secondary Species]] — but that is a question of whether it still exists, not of what it is.)

## Worked example: the Uchiha

- **Clan** — the standing classification, and the one to keep. The Sharingan is tied to Uchiha genealogy and Uchiha social identity.
- **Several Clans** — if the family fragments, descendants drop the name, and separate houses independently preserve the trait. Still not a subtype.
- **Racial Subtype** — only once Sharingan-related biology exists across enough independent populations that calling every bearer an Uchiha descendant stops making sense, *and* the inheritance has broadened past the eye: distinctive ocular development, specialized brain structure, altered aura pathways, predictable neurological response, unusual visual perception even before awakening. At that point "Uchiha-type Human" names a body pattern, not a family. A million people with nothing but the Sharingan in common would still be a very large collection of Clans.
- **Species** — only if the entire developmental baseline diverges: universal ocular structures, distinct neurological development, its own default aura pathways, stable reproductive biology, a body that reliably produces the complete Uchiha-derived form.

The Clan reading is the one this vault uses, and deliberately so. The Sharingan is more interesting as an exceptionally powerful inheritance concentrated inside one volatile family than as a race.
