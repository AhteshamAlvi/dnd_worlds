A creature's Aura reserve is tracked through three primary characteristics — **Maximum Aura Pool**, **Aura Output**, and **Aura Regeneration** — plus a fourth, changing value, **Current Aura Pool**, that is derived from the first rather than being an independent statistic of its own.

## Maximum Aura Pool

Maximum Aura Pool is the total quantity of Aura that makes up a creature's usable life-force reserve — it answers *how much Aura does this organism possess in total?* It depends on both **Constitution (CON)** and **Vitality (VIT)** jointly: the strength of a creature's life force depends on the body's ability to contain and withstand that life force (CON) and on the organism's underlying vitality (VIT) alike, so the two attributes determine the pool's magnitude together rather than separately. A normal human at roughly CON 10 / VIT 10 is the baseline this scales from, sitting at a Maximum Aura Pool of about 10 — see [[The Standard Slime]] for what that quantity actually means. The exact mathematical relationship between CON, VIT, and pool size beyond that baseline has not yet been fixed.

Maximum Aura Pool is measured on the same [[The Standard Slime|Standard Slime]] energy scale as Natural Energy and Life Force.

## Current Aura Pool

Current Aura Pool is how much of a creature's total Aura remains available at a given moment. It is not calculated independently from attributes — it is simply bounded between zero and the Maximum Aura Pool, falling as Aura is expended and rising as Aura regenerates, and it can never exceed the maximum. A character might have a Maximum Aura Pool of 10 and a Current Aura Pool of 6.4: their life force is capable of sustaining up to 10, but only 6.4 of it is presently available to draw on. What happens at very low or zero Current Aura Pool is not yet defined.

## Aura Output

Aura Output is how much Aura a body is physically capable of forcing into active use at once. Its limit is set by the strain that releasing Aura places on the body — greater releases produce greater physical backlash, and a body with greater Constitution can tolerate more of that release before the strain becomes unsafe. Aura Output therefore depends primarily on **CON**.

This makes Output distinct from Maximum Aura Pool in a real way: a creature can carry an enormous reserve while having comparatively poor Output, if its body can't withstand releasing much of that reserve at once, and conversely a creature with a more modest Pool can have unusually high Output if its Constitution lets its body withstand intense release. Maximum Aura Pool answers *how much Aura do I have*; Aura Output answers *how much of it can my body actually bring to bear*.

## Aura Regeneration

Aura Regeneration is how quickly expended Aura is replenished, and its primary attribute is **VIT**. Vitality governs healing, biological recovery, and the restoration of life force generally, and Aura regeneration is one more expression of that same capacity to renew. A creature with high VIT replenishes Aura quickly even if its Constitution caps how violently it can release that Aura in the first place; conversely, high CON with low VIT can mean enormous Output paired with comparatively slow recovery from spending it.

## How the three relate

Each statistic describes a different physical property of the same underlying Aura system, and each is rooted in a different attribute:

- **Maximum Aura Pool** — total reserve, from CON *and* VIT together.
- **Aura Output** — maximum usable release, constrained by physical strain, from CON.
- **Aura Regeneration** — the rate expended Aura is restored, from VIT.

This lets creatures with comparable total reserves function very differently. A high-CON, low-VIT creature carries a strong pool, a very high Output, and slow regeneration; a low-CON, high-VIT creature can carry a comparably strong pool with lower Output but fast regeneration. Their total life-force strength may be similar, since Maximum Aura Pool draws on both attributes — but how their bodies actually interact with that Aura, moment to moment, differs dramatically.
