Nenworld replaces ordinary d20 advancement with **three tracks that rise separately**. Characters have levels, but level is not a measure of combat power and is not where abilities come from. Overall level, individual attributes, and per-principle Nen mastery each advance on their own resource, and each is gated by fiction as well as by arithmetic.

| Track | Maximum | Resource | Represents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Character Level** | 30 | Experience (XP) | Overall experience and development |
| **Attributes** | 30 each | Stat Points (SP) | Physical, mental, social, and spiritual capability |
| **Nen Mastery** | Mastery X per principle | Growth Points (GP) | Command of one specific [[0 Nen Principles|principle]] or [[Hatsu]] |

**The tracks do not convert.** XP cannot be spent on attributes or mastery. Stat Points cannot buy mastery. Growth Points cannot raise an attribute. A level-up hands you both SP and GP; where each goes is a separate decision.

So level does not describe a character. Two level-20 characters can share nothing: one with DEX and CON driven high and a fistful of combat principles, another with INT, WIS, PER, CHA, a complicated [[Hatsu]], deep knowledge, and far more use outside a fight.

**Growth is still grown, not granted.** Every spend needs an in-fiction justification — training time, a teacher, a breakthrough, or the Do → Notice → Propose → Justify loop from [[Developing a Nen Ability]]. The currencies are permission *plus* pacing; fiction is the gate. And **you never buy aura directly:** max pool is derived from CON ([[Aura Statistics]]), so it grows when CON grows.

## 1 · Character Level

Thirty levels. Each costs more than the last — quick early, punishing late.

> **XP for the next level = 5 + 0.75L + L³ / 75**, where **L** is your current level. The raw result is rounded to one significant figure before it's added to the total.

| Advancement | XP | | Advancement | XP |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 → 2 | 6 | | 20 → 21 | 100 |
| 5 → 6 | 10 | | 25 → 26 | 200 |
| 10 → 11 | 30 | | 29 → 30 | 400 |
| 15 → 16 | 60 | | **1 → 30 total** | **3,000** |

Cumulative: **100 XP** to reach level 10, **700** to reach level 20, **3,000** to reach level 30.

Every Level grants **2 Stat Points and 3 Growth Points, including Level 1.** *(Design invention — the spec left the per-level figure open; the derivation is in [[Balance and Math]] §13. Adjust both together.)*

Levelling raises nothing by itself. It hands you resources to invest later, when the fiction supports it.

**Bands.** The power tiers this book uses elsewhere map to level roughly as: **1–5** fodder · **6–12** licensed and working · **13–20** professional · **21–26** elite · **27–30** the edge of mastery. Treat this as description, never as a gate — [[Canon Benchmarks]] and the actual numbers on the sheet decide what a character can survive.

### Post-Cap XP Milestones

Character Level stops at 30, but Lifetime XP never resets and keeps accumulating past it. The same curve above simply keeps running as a bookkeeping fiction beyond Level 30, without ever moving actual Level again. Every five of those continuing "formula levels" past 30 marks a **Post-Cap XP Milestone**, worth **+1 Stat Point and +3 Growth Points**:

| Milestone | Equivalent formula Level | Lifetime XP threshold | Cumulative post-cap SP | Cumulative post-cap GP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| I | 35 | 5,400 | 61 | 93 |
| II | 40 | 9,000 | 62 | 96 |
| III | 45 | 13,900 | 63 | 99 |

A character with 9,000 Lifetime XP is still Level 30 on the sheet, but has banked two Post-Cap Milestones' worth of extra SP and GP beyond the 60 SP / 90 GP that Level 30 alone provides. This gives a character who keeps adventuring after hitting the cap somewhere for that continued XP to go, instead of every further success being wasted. *(Design invention, not in the original spec — [[Balance and Math]] §13.)*

## 2 · Earning XP

XP is meaningful development, not corpses. It comes from quests, battles, exploration, discoveries, training, investigation, social achievement, survival, personal breakthroughs, hard decisions, and campaign events.

**A quest need not pay every currency.** One grants XP and Growth Points. Another grants only Stat Points. Another grants no XP at all but satisfies a [[Nen Growth|Mastery Catalyst]]. Some pay in equipment, money, reputation, knowledge, allies, access, or consequences and nothing else.

### Combat XP — the Development Value

A fight pays when it *develops* the character. Winning is neither necessary nor sufficient. Score five factors:

| Factor | Range | The question |
|---|---:|---|
| **Pressure** | 0–4 | How hard did this push them, relative to what they can currently do? |
| **Novelty** | 0–2 | Unfamiliar opponent, power, tactic, environment, or Nen interaction? |
| **Adaptation** | 0–2 | Did they have to change strategy, aura distribution, teamwork, or principle use? |
| **Stakes** | 0–1 | Did failure carry real consequence? |
| **Insight** | 0–1 | Did they learn something, or find a weakness that matters? |

The total is the encounter's **Development Value**, awarded as XP. A routine fight is worth **0**. A serious one runs 3–6. A campaign-defining, multi-stage battle can be scored as several encounters or allowed past the 10-point ceiling.

This is not meant to turn every skirmish into arithmetic. It is a consistent basis for the one question that matters: *did this fight actually grow them?*

**First contact with a new enemy** usually pays, because it carries Novelty — but novelty alone guarantees nothing. Erasing an unfamiliar monster before it acts teaches nothing. A familiar rival returning with better technique, new conditions, and a read on the character can be worth more than anything else in the arc. The rule is not "first kill pays." It is:

> First exposure can contribute Novelty. XP comes from meaningful development.

**Losing pays.** A character can learn more from losing to a superior opponent than from beating five weak ones. A defeat earns full XP when they made a real effort, felt real pressure, found a genuine weakness, tried to adapt, or came away with information they can use. What cannot be farmed is throwing yourself at an unbeatable enemy without changing anything.

**Repetition decays rather than switching off.** First meaningful exposure pays full Novelty; a repeat with some new wrinkle pays reduced but real XP; the same solved problem pays nothing. When the opponent evolves, the ground changes, the stakes rise, or the character tries a fundamentally different approach, the encounter becomes valuable again.

And a fight can pay in something other than XP: a Catalyst satisfied, a new Hatsu option revealed, Growth Points, a Limited Stat Point, access to a teacher.

## 3 · Stat Points

Every attribute maxes at **30**, but only eight of the ten are raised this way: **STR, AGI, DEX, CON, VIT, INT, WIS, PER**. **SPI and CHA are rolled separately at character creation and cannot be increased by ordinary Stat Points at all** — the only way either one moves afterward is a Limited Stat Point grant (below).

### Starting array

The eight ordinary attributes begin from a fixed Level 1 array, assigned freely across them:

> **11, 11, 10, 10, 10, 10, 9, 9** — total 80, average 10.

SPI and CHA are not part of this array; each is decided by a separate roll at character creation instead. *(Open item: the exact roll — die, modifiers, floor/ceiling — is not yet specified. Racial adjustments and the old creation caps of 16 for SPI / 18 post-race assumed the previous 15-point standard array and need to be revisited against these lower starting values before they're treated as settled.)*

### Raising a stat

> **1 Stat Point = +1 to one of the eight ordinary Attributes, always.**

The cost never rises with the current score — 1 SP takes an attribute from 10 to 11 exactly as cheaply as from 29 to 30. What keeps 30 out of easy reach is not the price of the last point, it's the size of the budget: taking one attribute from 10 to 30 costs **20 SP**, about a third of everything levelling alone ever pays (**60 SP** by Level 30). Taking all eight ordinary attributes the same distance would cost **160 SP** against that same 60 SP career income, and SPI/CHA can't be pushed there through this track at all. **30 is a pinnacle, not the expected endpoint of a high-level character** — a character can master a principle completely without ever reaching 30 in its governing stat ([[Nen Growth|mastery X]] asks for 28, which costs 18 SP from the 10 starting point). *(Design invention — the spec left the per-point cost open; an earlier draft used an escalating cost curve, replaced with a flat cost so the fixed SP supply does the limiting instead. Derivation in [[Balance and Math]] §13.)*

**Fiction gates still apply** ([[Nen Growth]] for rates): physical attributes want an 8-week regimen, mental and social ones study or immersion, and **CON** the aura-tempering discipline that also grows the pool. SPI cannot be trained through ordinary Stat Points at all — see Limited Stat Points below.

### Limited Stat Points

A quest, event, item, special mechanic, or direct GM grant can permanently increase one **explicitly named** Attribute — a **Limited STR Point**, a **Limited SPI Point**, **+2 CON**, and so on. A Limited Stat Point grant:

- does not consume or add to the ordinary Stat Point balance;
- may target any Attribute, including SPI and CHA — this is the only way either one increases after character creation;
- remains subject to the normal Attribute cap of 30.

Because ordinary Stat Points now cost a flat 1 SP per point, a Limited Stat Point grant is simply a full, free point handed straight to a named Attribute rather than the pool. It should stay correspondingly rare, tied to something that actually happened in the fiction — not a routine purchase.

**Skill, attack, and save proficiencies** are bought with Stat Points at **2 / 4 / 6 / 8** for Trained / Expert / Master / Legendary, with the same fiction gates as before (practice, 8 weeks' use, a breakthrough moment, GM invitation). *(Design decision: the spec does not price skills. Attributes and skills are the same family of personal capability, so they share a currency — flag for veto if you'd rather they had their own.)*

## 4 · Growth Points

Growth Points buy **Nen mastery and nothing else**. They come from level-ups, quests, training rewards, major events, and relevant accomplishments, and may be banked indefinitely.

Once committed toward a rank attempt, GP are **attached to that attempt** and cannot be moved to another discipline. The full schedule, the stat thresholds, the Catalysts, the Breakthrough rolls, and the foundation caps are in **[[Nen Growth]]**; the per-principle stat requirements are in **[[Nen Principles (Rules)]]**.

The one number worth carrying here: taking a single principle from nothing to Mastery X costs **113 GP** — but the [[Nen Growth|foundation cap]] means an *advanced* principle at X drags every prerequisite to X with it, so the cheapest complete advanced mastery ([[Fū]], via [[Ten]], [[Ren]], [[En]], [[Hatsu]]) is five principles and **565 GP before a single failed Breakthrough**. Levelling alone pays **90** by Level 30 (2 SP + 3 GP per level, [[Balance and Math]] §13) — not even enough to nominal-cover one ordinary principle to X (90/113, 80%), let alone an advanced one. Quests, training rewards, and events carry the overwhelming majority of any real mastery path now. **One total mastery is still the reward for a campaign spent chasing it; all fifteen would cost 1,695 and is not a thing anyone does.** Specialization is not encouraged by this system; it is enforced by it.

⚠ **The Breakthrough expected-value table and the "levels alone reach 43%" framing below in [[Balance and Math]] §13 were tuned against the old 12 GP/level supply (348 GP by Level 30) and are now stale** — under the current 3 GP/level (90 GP by Level 30), the mastery-cost curve and the Breakthrough modifier table need a dedicated rebalance pass, not just a number swap. Flagged there, not fixed there.

**Control ranks** are not a principle and have no mastery track, but they are Nen proficiency, so they are bought with GP: **2 / 4 / 7 / 11** for Control 2 / 3 / 4 / 5, gates unchanged ([[Nen Growth]]). Control 6 remains untrainable for baseline humans. *(Design addition — the spec does not cover Control.)*

## 5 · Quest and event rewards

Rewards should reflect what was actually accomplished.

| Reward | Function |
|---|---|
| XP | Advances Character Level |
| Stat Points | Investable in any of the eight ordinary attributes |
| Limited Stat Points | Raise one named attribute immediately, bypassing the ordinary pool — very rare, only route for SPI/CHA |
| Growth Points | Investable toward Nen mastery |
| Mastery Catalyst | Satisfies the narrative gate for one specific mastery rank |
| Breakthrough bonus | Improves a future Breakthrough Check |
| Principle awakening | Unlocks a principle the character could not previously train |
| Teacher or training access | Permits specialized advancement |
| Hatsu development | A new ability, form, mode, or condition |
| Technique or martial skill | A non-Nen move or discipline |
| Equipment, knowledge, reputation, allies | Non-progression rewards |

Hauling a sealed monolith across hostile country pays **Limited STR Points**. Studying under an aura theorist pays **INT Points**, Growth Points, or access to [[Fū]] training. Surviving sustained hostile [[Ren]] satisfies a [[Ten]] or [[Ken]] Catalyst. Unpicking a stranger's Hatsu pays XP and an INT-flavored insight and no physical development whatsoever.

## 6 · Training programs

A program = **focus + duration + weekly check + success threshold** ([[Awakening and the Path]] shows the format). Time can run alongside adventuring at **standard** intensity; **dedicated** work needs real downtime.

- **Weekly check:** the program's listed attribute or skill; DC 13 baseline, DC 10 with a competent teacher, DC 16 self-taught on novel material.
- **Successes needed:** scale with the target — 2–3 for an early rank, rising to 6+ near the top. Failed weeks cost time, not progress.
- **Partial success:** half the successes when time runs out banks them; resume without restarting.
- **Nat 1 self-taught:** 1 Strain ([[Injury Recovery and Conditions]]) — you pushed something wrong.
- **Fatigue:** past 12 consecutive dedicated weeks without rest, 1 Strain per further week. Masters schedule slack.

**Downtime throughput limit:** per downtime block, at most **two principle rank attempts + one Control rank**, *or* **one Hatsu rank attempt**, *or* one attribute regimen. Aura growth weeks tick alongside regardless. (Prevents downtime-stuffing; matches the lore's insistence that Nen is long discipline, not a shopping trip.)

| Program | Duration & check | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Awaken (meditation)** | 8 wks teacher / 16 solo; CON (Endurance) DC 13/16; 4 successes | [[Awakening and the Path]] |
| **Ten → Ren, first ranks** | 1–2 wks each; listed there | The shared foundation |
| **Aura Control drills** | 12 wks; DEX DC 13; 4 successes | Coin-hover, leaf-split, bowl-stilling |
| **Expanding En** | 8 wks; CON (Endurance) DC 16; 4 successes | Hold radius +1 m past comfort daily; Strain on overrun is expected |
| **Category bell-curve block** | 4 wks mixed categories | Grants the ×1.25 primary bonus ([[Nen Growth]]) |
| **First Hatsu (seed → form 1)** | 2 wks + design table-time; SPI DC 13; 2 successes | [[Developing a Nen Ability]] worksheet, then the [[Hatsu Design]] audit |
| **Water Divination retreat** | 1 wk | DIY divination is a rite, not a formality — run the scene |

### Solo, supervised, observed, stolen

- **Supervised:** teacher multipliers ([[Nen Growth]]) — the main axis of quality.
- **Solo:** ×2 time, DC +3, nat-1 Strain. Heroic, slow, occasionally crippling.
- **Observation:** watching a principle in live use 3+ times + INT (Nen Theory) DC 16 → ×¾ time when you train it later. Watching a *Hatsu* teaches you its Conditions if you win the reads, never the ability.
- **Stolen methods:**

| Method | Gain | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Forced Initiation "before they're ready" | Skip the awakening program | The full [[Awakening and the Path]] risk table; reputations end this way |
| Aura stimulants (black-market) | One week counts double | CON save DC 16 or 2 Strain; addiction track on repeats |
| Leech-training (master channels aura *through* student) | CON regimens complete ×2 faster for the block | Dependency: −10% AO without the master nearby, until a clean season passes |
| Crucible past sane limits (unsanctioned death games) | CON regimens ×2 faster and a principle-time discount | Real death chance every week — [[Society Law and Licenses|and it's illegal]] |

## 7 · Montages and time skips

Set the calendar, apply [[Nen Growth]] rates honestly, cap by the throughput limit per *narrated block* (a one-year skip ≈ 4 blocks). Roll one representative program check per block, player's choice where the drama was. Print the before/after sheet — the visible jump is the reward.

Outlined here, priced elsewhere:

- **Desperation Surge** (+50% AO, 1/arc, 2 Strain after) — [[Injury Recovery and Conditions]]

## 9 · Progression yardsticks

Expected values, not gates. XP totals assume the Development Value scoring above. ⚠ These in-fiction time estimates predate the current XP curve's higher 3,000-total-to-30 (§1, up from an earlier 1,310) and haven't been individually rechecked against it — treat them as rough until someone re-times a few sample arcs against the new curve.

| Stage | Level | In-fiction time | CON → AP (derived) | Ren / Control | Hatsu |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Creation | 1 | — | — (not yet awakened) | — | — |
| First-arc end | 3–5 | 3–4 months | CON 13–14 → 120–200 | II / 1 | II (seed) |
| Year one | 6–10 | 1 year | CON 15–16 → 315–500 | IV / 2 | IV, first form |
| Professional | 13–20 | 2–4 years | CON 17–19 → 800–2k | IV–VI / 2–3 | IV–VI |
| Elite | 21–26 | 8–15 years | CON 20–22 → 3.3k–8.4k | VIII / 4 | VI–VIII |
| Master | 27–30 | 20+ years | CON 23–25 → 13k–35k | X / 5 | VIII–X |

Elite users are rare because the arithmetic says so. Reaching elite means driving CON deep into the superhuman band — a dozen hard-won regimens at doubling time-cost past the mundane cap, across a decade of *sustained* dedication — while spending a GP budget that can only ever cover a fraction of the Path, and surviving everything that hunts the promising. No wall stops anyone; the years, the costs, and the danger do the filtering. The world's masters are the survivors of that triple filter. Treat every one as a named institution.
