Giants are a people built on a scale nothing else walking on two legs matches. The common lineages stand roughly five times the height of a man; the old true-blooded lines are twice that again, tall enough that one sitting down still looks over a rooftop. They are not [[Monsters]] and not a [[Variant Tribes|variant tribe]] of humans — they are their own species, with homelands, crafts, laws, faiths, and a very long memory of who has wronged them.

Their strength is not a power and costs them nothing. It comes from simply being that much creature: mass, reach, and bone. An untrained Giant laborer can do things no trained human fighter can, and a Giant who *has* trained is less an opponent than a change in the terrain. The same mass makes them extraordinarily hard to put down — wounds that would kill a person outright are, on that body, an injury to be walked off and complained about later.

Giants also live a very long time, several times a human span, and this shapes everything about them. A grudge can outlast the nation that caused it. An apprenticeship can run longer than a human career. A war can be prosecuted at a pace that looks, to shorter-lived peoples, like geology.

## Elbaf

The great Giant nation is **Elbaf**, and it is a warrior culture in the strictest sense: not a militarised state, but a people for whom honour in combat is the organising principle of life, law, and religion alike. Disputes are settled by single combat rather than by court or council. Both parties are expected to fight fairly, to accept the outcome, and to bear no grudge afterward — and dying on your feet in a fair fight is understood as the best end available to anyone.

Elbaf's warriors take that literally enough to be genuinely unnerving to outsiders. Two Giants who cannot resolve a disagreement will duel it out, and if the duel is inconclusive they will simply fight it again — every day, for as long as it takes, which given Giant lifespans can mean a century of daily combat over a question nobody else considers important. Neither considers this absurd. Neither considers stopping.

The flip side is an absolute contempt for cowardice, poison, ambush, and anything else that wins without risking yourself. A people who will not use those tools are structurally vulnerable to those who will, and Giant history is full of Elbaf warriors being beaten by much weaker enemies willing to be dishonest about it.

Elbaf's faith and its warrior code are the same institution, and Giants abroad tend to keep both — a Giant far from home still fights by Elbaf's rules even where nobody around them has heard of Elbaf.

## Earth-shapers

Not every Giant tradition is Elbaf's. Older mountain-dwelling Giant peoples built their culture around **earth and stone** instead of the duel — raising terrain, shaping rock into wall and weapon, and making the ground itself do the work. This is craft and inherited technique rather than a racial power, and it is old enough that most human earth-shaping traditions are borrowed from Giants rather than invented independently.

Where Elbaf produces soldiers, these lineages produce builders, and the two branches regard each other with the mild, permanent condescension of any two schools that both think the other has missed the point.

## Giants abroad

Because a single Giant is worth a company of anyone else, Giant mercenaries and Giant soldiers turn up everywhere — as pirate crews, as royal guards, and in the ranks of the great navies, where a Giant officer is a standing argument that the institution is serious. Most are working, not exiled; Elbaf treats going out to fight for someone as an ordinary career, provided the fighting is honest.

Size is not uniform across the race, and the gradient is the usual source of friction within it. Smaller Giant lines can live in human cities uneasily, while true Giants keep to their own territory for the simple reason that there is nowhere in a human settlement for them to stand. The smaller lines are frequently treated as diminished rather than merely different, which they resent.

Giants also interbreed with other peoples where biology allows. The **Imuchakk** of the frozen north are the clearest case — a hybrid people descended from Giant and [[Merfolk]] unions, carrying the size of one parent race and the water-affinity of the other.

## Ancient Giants

A separate and far older lineage, now effectively extinct: Giants larger again than the true-blooded lines, of a scale that puts them closer to the great [[Monsters]] than to their own descendants. What survives of them is mostly corpses, legends, and the occasional preserved specimen that somebody unwise decides to reanimate. They are not a living culture and should not be treated as one.

## Giants and Nen

Like any race here, Giants can learn [[0 Nen|Nen]], and it behaves differently on that body than on a human one. There is far more of them for aura to cover, so a Giant's power spreads thin across its own bulk unless deliberately concentrated — which means a Giant who never learned to focus is exactly the "enormous, powerful, and beatable by a smaller precise opponent" the stories promise. A Giant who *did* learn to concentrate is proportionally catastrophic, because the same body that diluted its aura can also hold a nation's worth of it behind one fist.
