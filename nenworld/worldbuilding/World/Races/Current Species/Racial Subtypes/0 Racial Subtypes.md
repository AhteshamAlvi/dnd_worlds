A **Racial Subtype** is what a variation becomes once it is no longer meaningfully contained inside one [[0 Clans|Clan]]. Its members are still [[Humans|Human]] — same species, same underlying development — but they carry a stable secondary biological or aura pattern that has become *normal* across an entire population. The trait has stopped being "what this family inherits" and become "what people from here are born as."

The five conditions, in full, are in [[Classification]]. In short: several reproductively independent lineages, traits that breed reliably, no ritual or exposure needed to produce them, a population that sustains itself through its own births, and a *package* of differences rather than one isolated ability. The demographic floor — 1,000 breeding members across 5 independent lineages for 10 generations — exists only so that one unusually large family cannot qualify on headcount alone.

That fifth condition is what keeps this tier small. A single inherited ability, however spectacular, stays a Clan bloodline: a million people with the Sharingan and nothing else in common would be a million [[Uchiha]]-descended Clan members, not a race. What earns a place here is a body that is *built differently* across the board.

## Two calls worth revisiting

Both entries were moved here rather than written here, so the reasoning is worth stating plainly:

**[[Fanalis]]** were previously filed as a Clan, and their own note still calls them one. They are moved because their trait is a physiological package — build, speed, strength, senses, colouring — carried by a *people* across scattered independent communities rather than by one traceable family. What has never been settled is their headcount: the note describes Fanalis society as "small, scattered," which is exactly the thing the 1,000-member floor is meant to test. If that population is genuinely below the floor, they belong back in [[0 Clans|Clans]].

**[[Variant Tribes]]** sit on the other edge. Each tribe breeds true for a *single* trait, which reads against the "more than one narrow ability" condition — but the trait is anatomical and developmental rather than a Nen ability, it defines a whole tribe instead of a family, and there are many such tribes. Every structural test points here; only the one-trait test argues otherwise.
